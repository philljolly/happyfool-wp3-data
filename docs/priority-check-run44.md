## Corrections to the run-43 table

- **Steering Awareness v3**: random vectors at test on trained models appear twice, not once. As well as the Gaussian 94% rejection, Table 5 lists "Random direction 0 ± 0%" detection (cos 0.002). The causal-injection test also uses norm-matched noise (P(detect)=0.309). So "no prior work tests trained models under random-vector injection at test" is **false as worded**. Fonseca Rivera & Africa do this, and their trained model *rejects* random vectors, because random vectors were labelled negatives in training. The claim must be narrowed: no prior work tests a trained *localisation* (which-sentence) report under random injection, and no prior trained report was trained without random negatives and then tested on them.
- **Hahami IFT**: the 950/50 split is confirmed (§D.3 "950-concept training split", §D.6 "50 held-out concepts"). Their training prefill is an affirmative statement ("Yes, I detect an injected thought. It is located in SENTENCE"). Appendix B contradicts the main text. The main text says the yes/no paradigm is confounded (r=0.999), but App. B concludes: "This confirms that the model’s “Yes” responses to the detection prompt reflect genuine sensitivity to the injected activation pattern, not a general affirmative bias." This looks like an internal error. Don't cite App. B's conclusion.
- **Macar**: run 43's "none at injection" is confirmed. The only random-direction control is for abliteration.

## Verdict

Among the 12 works checked (10 read in full text), none asks a trained introspective report a polarity-reversed question. The reversed-question test still appears new. The random-vector part of the claim must be narrowed. Fonseca Rivera & Africa (2511.21399v3) do test trained models with norm-matched Gaussian and random-direction vectors at test (94% rejection, 0% detection), but random negatives were in their training data, and their task is yes/no, not localisation. A safe wording is: "no prior work tests a trained localisation report under a reversed question, or under random-vector injection when random vectors were absent from training." The nearest relative in *paradigm* is still Hahami et al.'s IFT (2607.14111): same "which sentence has the injected thought" task, LoRA-trained, held-out concepts, Gaussian only as a training arm. Their claim that localisation is "immune to global affirmative bias" is exactly what our Q = .00 result challenges. The nearest relative in *logic* is the LawZero paper (2604.17465). Its §4.2 control is built to rule out a model that "disproportionally picks a perturbed sentence, no matter the question", which is precisely the failure our adapter shows. But it uses untrained models and a content question, not a reversed one. Zou et al. (2609.35108) add a useful baseline: untrained Qwen3-4B-IT localises norm-matched Gaussian directions at 0.84%. That supports the reading that the adapter's pull towards whatever sentence was perturbed came from training.
